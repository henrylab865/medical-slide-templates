structured-abstract

Structured Abstract

Background

Under-triage in pediatric emergency departments can lead to serious adverse outcomes. Evidence supporting AI-assisted triage remains limited.

Methods

Parallel-group RCT conducted at eight sites in Japan. We randomized 235 pediatric emergency department patients aged 1 month to 15 years to AI-assisted triage (n=120) or conventional triage (n=115).

Results

The primary outcome, under-triage, occurred in 7.7% of the AI group versus 12.4% of controls (risk difference −4.7%, 95% CI −7.8 to −1.6; p=0.003). Emergency department length of stay was also shorter (1.9 vs 3.2 h; p<0.001).

Conclusions

AI-assisted triage significantly reduced under-triage and may improve safety in pediatric emergency care. International multicenter studies are needed to assess generalizability.

Eligibility Criteria

Inclusion Criteria

  • Children aged 1 month to 15 years

Exclusion Criteria

  • Transferred after triage at another facility
  • Parent unable to understand the consent materials
study-design

Study Design Overview

Prospective Multicenter RCT

  • Design: Multicenter parallel-group randomized controlled trial
  • Sites: 8 sites (4 university, 3 children's, 1 community hospital)
  • Allocation: 1:1 central allocation, stratified by site and age
  • Blinding: Outcome assessors blinded
  • Enrollment: April 2024–March 2025
  • Follow-up: 28 days

statistical-methods

Statistical Analysis

Primary analysis

Intention-to-treat analysis

All randomized patients were analyzed in their assigned groups. The primary outcome (under-triage) was evaluated using risk difference with a two-sided α of 0.05.

Secondary analysis

Logistic regression

Multivariable analysis of factors associated with under-triage, adjusted for age, arrival time, acuity, and site; ORs with 95% CIs reported.

Sensitivity analysis

Multiple imputation (MICE)

Missing data were multiply imputed (m=20); robustness was checked against complete-case analysis.

learning-objectives

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Define pediatric febrile neutropenia (FN) and describe the initial approach
  2. Use MASCC and CISNE tools to distinguish low- from high-risk disease
  3. Justify empiric antibiotic selection using guideline recommendations
  4. Identify low-risk patients who may be managed as outpatients
key-definitions

Key Definitions

Febrile Neutropenia (FN)

Absolute neutrophil count < <500/μL (or expected to fall to < <500/μL within 48 hours) with an axillary temperature ≥37.5°C.

MASCC score

Risk score developed by the Multinational Association for Supportive Care in Cancer. A score ≥21 indicates low risk (sensitivity 71%; specificity 68%).

Empiric therapy

Broad-spectrum antibiotics are started before the pathogen is identified, then de-escalated according to culture results.

Breakthrough Infection

An infection that develops during prophylactic antibiotics, raising concern for resistant organisms and the need to modify therapy.
